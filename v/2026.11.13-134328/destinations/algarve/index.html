About Algarve

The Algarve is the southernmost region of mainland Portugal, a strip of coast and hills between the Atlantic and the Alentejo plains. Faro is its capital and main airport. West of Faro, the coast breaks into ochre limestone cliffs, rock stacks and small coves; east of Faro, it opens into long sandy islands sheltering the Ria Formosa lagoon.

Most visitors come for the beaches and the sunshine: Faro records about 3,000 hours of sun a year, and summer days are usually dry and warm without the heat of inland Iberia. The sea is the Atlantic, so it is fresher than the Mediterranean, at around 21 °C in late summer.

Away from the resorts, the Algarve has Moorish castles, whitewashed hill villages, cork-oak and eucalyptus hills around Monchique and a fishing culture that still shapes what you eat. Spring and autumn are the easiest seasons for walking, sightseeing and quieter beaches, and winter is mild enough for golf and coastal walks most days.

When to go to Algarve

The best time to visit the Algarve is May to June or September, when it is warm and sunny, the sea is swimmable and the beaches are calmer than in summer. July and August are hot, dry and busiest. Winter is mild and quiet, good for walking and golf, but some days are wet.

Best things to do in Algarve

  1. 011. See the rock arches at Ponta da Piedade

    Ponta da Piedade, just south of Lagos, is a headland of honey-coloured rock stacks, arches and grottoes. Walk the clifftop paths to the lighthouse, take the steps down to the water, or see the grottoes from a small boat or kayak from Lagos marina.

  2. 022. Visit the Benagil sea cave by boat

    The Benagil cave (Algar de Benagil), near Lagoa, is a domed sea cave with a hole in its roof and a small beach inside. Since 2024 you can only see it from the water on a licensed boat trip or guided kayak tour; swimming in and landing on the beach are not allowed.

  3. 033. Walk the Seven Hanging Valleys Trail

    The Seven Hanging Valleys Trail (Percurso dos Sete Vales Suspensos) runs about 6 km along the clifftops between Praia da Marinha and Praia de Vale Centeanes, near Carvoeiro. It passes Benagil and views over sea caves and arches. Start early in summer: there is little shade.

  4. 044. Explore Tavira and its island beach

    Tavira is the eastern Algarve’s prettiest town, with a pedestrian bridge over the River Gilão, church towers and a ruined castle with views over the rooftops. Ferries cross to Ilha de Tavira, a long sandy island beach in the Ria Formosa.

  5. 055. Take a boat into the Ria Formosa

    The Ria Formosa Natural Park is a lagoon of channels, salt marsh and barrier islands stretching east from Faro. Boats from Faro and Olhão cross to sandy islands such as Ilha Deserta, Culatra and Armona, and the marshes are good for birdwatching, including flamingos and spoonbills.

  6. 066. Stand at Cabo de São Vicente and Sagres

    Cabo de São Vicente, near Sagres, is the south-western tip of mainland Europe, with a lighthouse on sheer cliffs above the Atlantic. Nearby, the Fortaleza de Sagres sits on a windswept promontory. Come for sunset, and bring a layer: it is often windy.

  7. 077. Climb to Silves castle

    Silves was the capital of the Algarve under Moorish rule, and its red sandstone castle still crowns the hill above the town. Walk the walls, then visit the cathedral just below. Silves is about 20 minutes’ drive inland from Portimão.

  8. 088. Head into the hills at Monchique

    The Serra de Monchique is the Algarve’s green, wooded mountain range, and Fóia, at 902 m, is its highest point, with views to the coast on clear days. Monchique town has steep lanes and local crafts, and the spa village of Caldas de Monchique sits in a shaded valley below.

Hidden gems

  • Cacela VelhaCacela Velha is a tiny whitewashed village on a low cliff above the eastern end of the Ria Formosa, with a church and small fort. At low tide you can wade or take a small boat across to the sandbar beach opposite.
  • Ilha da CulatraIlha da Culatra is a car-free barrier island in the Ria Formosa, reached by ferry from Olhão. Its fishing village has sandy lanes and simple seafood restaurants, and a boardwalk leads to a long, quiet ocean beach.
  • AlteAlte, in the hills north of Loulé, is one of the Algarve’s best-kept villages, with white houses, a 16th-century church and the shaded springs of Fonte Grande and Fonte Pequena. It makes a cool half-day trip from the coast in summer.

Where to stay in Algarve

  • Lagos

    Good for: First-timersBeachesNightlife

    Lagos is a historic walled town in the western Algarve with a lively old centre, a marina and some of the region’s best coves, such as Praia Dona Ana and Praia do Camilo. It has a good choice of places to eat and drink and easy boat trips to Ponta da Piedade.

  • Albufeira

    Good for: FamiliesNightlifeBeaches

    Albufeira is the Algarve’s biggest resort, with a whitewashed old town above a wide town beach and many more beaches nearby. The Strip, in Areias de São João, is busy late into the night in summer, while the old town and beaches suit families by day.

  • Vilamoura

    Good for: LuxuryFamiliesCouples

    Vilamoura is a purpose-built resort around a large marina, about 20 km west of Faro, known for golf courses, restaurants on the waterfront and a long sandy beach. It is polished and practical rather than traditional.

  • Carvoeiro

    Good for: CouplesWalkersQuiet stays

    Carvoeiro is a former fishing village of white houses climbing the slopes around a small sandy cove, in the Lagoa area. It is a good base for the Seven Hanging Valleys Trail and the Benagil boat trips, and quieter in the evening than Albufeira.

  • Tavira

    Good for: CultureQuiet staysFood lovers

    Tavira is a low-rise riverside town in the eastern Algarve with old churches, tiled façades and a relaxed pace. Its beach is on Ilha de Tavira, reached by ferry, and the sea in the east is often calmer and a little warmer than in the west.

Food and drink in Algarve

Algarve cooking is built on the sea: grilled fish, shellfish and octopus, with almonds, figs and carob from the hills for sweets. Look for simple grills near the harbours, where the day’s catch is cooked over charcoal.

Dishes to try

Cataplana de marisco
A seafood stew of clams, prawns and fish with tomato, peppers and onion, cooked and served in a hinged copper pan called a cataplana. It is usually made for two people.
Sardinhas assadas
Fresh sardines grilled over charcoal and eaten with bread, boiled potatoes and salad. They are at their best in summer, when Portimão holds its sardine festival.
Frango piri-piri
Spatchcocked chicken grilled over charcoal and brushed with chilli oil. The village of Guia, near Albufeira, is especially known for it.
Polvo
Octopus is a speciality of Santa Luzia, near Tavira, where it is grilled, served in salads or cooked with rice as arroz de polvo.
Xerém com conquilhas
A soft cornmeal porridge cooked with small clams, a traditional Algarve dish often made with ham or chouriço too.
Doces de amêndoa
Small sweets of almond paste shaped and coloured like fruit, animals or flowers. Dom Rodrigo, threads of egg yolk and sugar wrapped in foil, is another Algarve sweet.

What to drink

Medronho
A strong clear spirit distilled from the fruit of the strawberry tree, made especially in the Monchique hills.
Amarguinha
A sweet bitter-almond liqueur from the Algarve, served chilled or over ice.
Algarve wine
The region has four wine areas, Lagos, Portimão, Lagoa and Tavira, producing mainly reds and rosés from small estates.

Culture and etiquette

The Algarve is relaxed and used to visitors, but Portuguese courtesy matters. A few words of Portuguese and a slower pace go a long way, especially in inland villages.

  • Say “bom dia” (good morning) or “boa tarde” (good afternoon) when you enter a shop or café.
  • Lunch is usually from about 12.30 to 3 pm and dinner from about 7.30 pm, later in summer.
  • Bread, olives and cheese put on the table before a meal are not free: you pay for what you eat, and you can send them back.
  • Cover up with a shirt or wrap when you leave the beach, and dress modestly in churches.
  • Portuguese is the language, not Spanish; “obrigado” (said by men) or “obrigada” (said by women) means thank you.

Getting to and around Algarve

Airports

  • FAOFaro AirportFaro Airport is the Algarve’s only international airport, about 4 km west of Faro city centre. Transfers take about 15 minutes to Faro, 25–30 minutes to Vilamoura, 40 minutes to Albufeira, 35 minutes to Tavira and around 1 hour 10 minutes to Lagos.

Getting around

Car rental
A hire car is the easiest way to reach beaches, villages and the hills. Portugal drives on the right. Tolls on the A22 coastal motorway (Via do Infante) were abolished in 2025, but other Portuguese motorways still have electronic tolls, so ask your hire company about a toll device.
Buses
Regional buses run by Vamus Algarve link the main towns along the coast and some inland villages. Services are frequent between the big resorts but sparse to smaller places and on Sundays.
Trains
The Algarve railway line runs along the coast from Lagos to Vila Real de Santo António via Portimão, Albufeira, Faro and Tavira. Some stations, including Albufeira, are a taxi or bus ride from the town centre.
Ferries and boats
Small ferries cross from Faro, Olhão and Tavira to the Ria Formosa islands. Times change with the season, so check them locally before you go.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Portugal for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before you arrive and be valid for at least 3 months after the day you plan to leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System records non-EU travellers’ fingerprints and photo at the border. EU citizens can travel with a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    Portugal uses the euro (EUR). Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for small cafés, markets and beach bars. Use cash machines marked Multibanco and choose to be charged in euros.

  • Plugs and power

    Portugal uses type C and F plugs with two round pins, at 230 V and 50 Hz. UK visitors need a UK-to-European adaptor.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is appreciated but not expected. Round up the bill in cafés and leave 5–10% in restaurants for good service.

  • Safety

    The Algarve is generally safe; watch for pickpockets in busy resorts. Obey beach flags (red means no swimming), keep away from the edges and bases of cliffs, which can collapse, and take care with strong Atlantic currents on the west coast.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a valid GHIC or EHIC for state healthcare, plus travel insurance with medical cover. The summer sun is strong: use high-factor sun cream, drink plenty of water and seek shade in the middle of the day.

  • Tap water

    Tap water in the Algarve is safe to drink. Some people prefer bottled water for the taste.

  • Accessibility

    Many beaches carry the national “Praia Acessível” label, with ramps or boardwalks and, at some, amphibious wheelchairs in summer. Old town centres such as Lagos and Albufeira have cobbled, sloping streets and steps, and many cliff beaches are reached only by stairs.

Algarve itineraries

3 days

Algarve in 3 days

A short trip along the central and western coast, with cliffs, caves and one historic town.

  1. Day 1Lagos and Ponta da PiedadeWalk the old town and walls of Lagos, then follow the clifftop path to Ponta da Piedade. Swim at Praia Dona Ana or Praia do Camilo and eat grilled fish near the harbour.
  2. Day 2Benagil and the Seven Hanging ValleysTake an early boat trip to the Benagil cave from Benagil, Carvoeiro or Portimão. In the late afternoon, walk part of the Seven Hanging Valleys Trail from Praia da Marinha.
  3. Day 3Silves and FaroVisit Silves castle and cathedral in the morning. Then drive to Faro for the old town inside its walls and a sunset view over the Ria Formosa.
7 days

Algarve in 7 days

A week from the wild west coast to the lagoons and towns of the east.

  1. Day 1LagosSettle in to Lagos, explore the old town and walk to Ponta da Piedade for the late light.
  2. Day 2Sagres and Cabo de São VicenteDrive west to the Fortaleza de Sagres and the lighthouse at Cabo de São Vicente. Stop at a west-coast beach such as Praia do Amado if the sea is calm.
  3. Day 3Monchique and SilvesHead into the hills to Fóia and Monchique, then down to Silves for the castle. Return via Caldas de Monchique.
  4. Day 4Benagil and CarvoeiroSee the Benagil cave by boat, walk the Seven Hanging Valleys Trail and spend the evening in Carvoeiro.
  5. Day 5Beach dayPick a long sandy beach such as Praia da Falésia, between Albufeira and Vilamoura, and take it easy.
  6. Day 6Faro and the Ria FormosaExplore Faro’s old town, then take a boat to Ilha Deserta or Culatra in the Ria Formosa Natural Park.
  7. Day 7Tavira and Cacela VelhaSpend the morning in Tavira and on Ilha de Tavira, then watch the sun go down from Cacela Velha.
