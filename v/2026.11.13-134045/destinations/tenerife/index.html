About Tenerife

Tenerife is a volcanic island in the Atlantic, about 300 km off the coast of Africa and around 4 hours 15 minutes by air from London. It is the largest and most populous of the Canary Islands, with almost a million residents, and it has been a winter-sun favourite for British travellers for decades.

The island has two characters. The dry, sunny south has the big resorts of Costa Adeje, Playa de las Américas and Los Cristianos, with calm beaches, promenades and plenty of family hotels. The greener north is cooler and cloudier, with the old towns of La Laguna, La Orotava and Puerto de la Cruz, banana plantations and laurel forest.

In between rises Mount Teide, at 3,715 m the highest peak in Spain, inside a national park that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. You can reach the summit area by cable car, walk lava fields above the clouds, swim with pilot whales offshore and eat grilled fish in a harbour village, all in one week.

At a glance

  • Tenerife is the largest of Spain’s Canary Islands, about 300 km off the coast of Africa.
  • Mount Teide on Tenerife is the highest mountain in Spain, at 3,715 m, and its national park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • The south of Tenerife is the sunniest and driest part of the island, with average highs of about 22 °C in January and 28 °C in August.
  • A direct flight from London to Tenerife takes about 4 hours 15 minutes, and the island keeps the same time as the UK all year.
  • Tenerife uses the euro, and UK visitors need a passport but no visa for stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

When to go to Tenerife

Tenerife is warm enough for the beach all year, which is why it is so popular for winter sun. The best all-round months are March–June and September–November, when it is warm, dry and quieter than the school holidays. July and August are the hottest and busiest months.

Best things to do in Tenerife

  1. 011. Go up Mount Teide

    Teide National Park is Tenerife’s main sight: a high volcanic crater filled with lava fields, rock towers and the 3,715 m peak of Mount Teide. A cable car climbs to 3,555 m, and the final path to the summit needs a free permit from the national park that is often booked up weeks ahead. Go early and bring warm layers.

  2. 022. Walk in the Anaga mountains

    The Anaga Rural Park in the north-east of Tenerife is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve of steep ridges, laurel forest and tiny villages. Marked trails start at the Cruz del Carmen visitor centre, about 20 minutes’ drive from La Laguna. Mornings are often misty, so go for the forest rather than guaranteed views.

  3. 033. Explore La Laguna

    San Cristóbal de La Laguna, the island’s former capital, has a grid of colonial streets that became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999. Its flat, partly pedestrianised centre is easy to walk, with a cathedral, painted mansions and lively student bars. The tram links it with Santa Cruz in about 40 minutes.

  4. 044. Hike the Masca gorge

    Masca is a mountain village in the Teno massif of north-west Tenerife, at the top of a deep ravine that runs down to the sea. Walking the Masca gorge trail must be booked in advance through the Cabildo de Tenerife and uses a compulsory shuttle bus. The road to the village itself is narrow and winding, with fine viewpoints on the way.

  5. 055. See whales and dolphins

    Short-finned pilot whales and bottlenose dolphins live all year in the strait between Tenerife and La Gomera. Boat trips leave from Los Cristianos, Puerto Colón in Costa Adeje and Los Gigantes, and many sail under the sheer cliffs of Los Gigantes, which rise several hundred metres from the sea. Choose an operator that follows the Canary Islands’ responsible whale-watching code.

  6. 066. Swim in the rock pools of Garachico

    Garachico is a small port on the north coast that was partly buried by lava in 1706. The cooled lava now forms El Caletón, a set of natural seawater pools with steps and walkways. The old town behind them has cobbled squares and a convent, and it makes an easy stop with La Orotava and Icod de los Vinos.

  7. 077. Wander La Orotava

    La Orotava sits above the Orotava valley in the north of Tenerife and is known for its 17th-century houses with carved wooden balconies. The steep old town has churches, terraced gardens and views down to the sea. It is about 15 minutes by car from Puerto de la Cruz.

  8. 088. Spend a day at Las Teresitas

    Playa de las Teresitas is a long beach of golden sand at San Andrés, about 8 km north-east of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, backed by the Anaga mountains. The sand was brought from the Sahara in the 1970s, and a breakwater keeps the water calm. It is popular with locals at weekends.

Hidden gems

  • Punta de TenoPunta de Teno is the western tip of Tenerife, with a lighthouse, clear water and views of the Los Gigantes cliffs. The road is often closed to private cars for much of the day, so check the rules before you go or take the local bus from Buenavista del Norte.
  • Benijo beachBenijo is a black-sand beach at the end of the road through Anaga, with sea stacks offshore and few buildings in sight. The sea is rough and there is often no lifeguard, so it is a place for sunset walks rather than swimming.
  • The Drago of Icod de los VinosIcod de los Vinos on the north coast is home to the Drago Milenario, a huge Canary Islands dragon tree that is several centuries old. It stands in a small paid garden, and the town around it has wine cellars and a pretty church square.

Where to stay in Tenerife

  • Los Cristianos

    Good for: FamiliesBeachesFirst-timers

    Los Cristianos grew from a fishing village and still has a working harbour, with ferries to La Gomera. Its two sheltered beaches and flat promenade make it a relaxed choice for families and older travellers.

  • Puerto de la Cruz

    Good for: CultureWalkersFood lovers

    Puerto de la Cruz is the north coast’s historic resort, with a fishing harbour, old squares and the Lago Martiánez lido designed by César Manrique. It is greener and cooler than the south and a good base for walking and touring.

  • Los Gigantes

    Good for: Quiet staysCouples

    Los Gigantes is a small, quieter resort on the west coast below sheer sea cliffs, with a marina, a black-sand beach and boat trips. It has some of the island’s best sunsets and is a short drive from Masca.

  • Costa Adeje

    Good for: FamiliesLuxuryBeaches

    Costa Adeje is the south-west’s smartest resort area, with sandy beaches such as Playa del Duque and Fañabé and a long seafront promenade. It has many large family and luxury hotels and is about 20 minutes from Tenerife South airport.

  • Playa de las Américas

    Good for: NightlifeBudgetFirst-timers

    Playa de las Américas is Tenerife’s busiest resort, with the island’s liveliest nightlife, surf breaks and plenty of good-value apartments. It joins Costa Adeje to the north and Los Cristianos to the south.

Food and drink in Tenerife

Canarian cooking is simple and filling, built on potatoes, fresh fish, goat’s cheese, gofio and mojo sauces. Look for menus that say “cocina canaria” and for guachinches, informal wine-growers’ eateries in the north.

Dishes to try

Papas arrugadas con mojo
Small potatoes boiled in their skins in very salty water until the skins wrinkle. They come with mojo rojo, a spicy red pepper sauce, and mojo verde, made with coriander or parsley.
Conejo en salmorejo
Rabbit marinated in garlic, vinegar, paprika and herbs, then stewed. It is one of Tenerife’s best-known traditional dishes.
Carne fiesta
Cubes of pork marinated in garlic, paprika and wine, then fried. It is a classic of village fiestas and guachinches.
Escaldón de gofio
Gofio, a flour of toasted grain eaten in the Canaries since before the Spanish conquest, mixed with hot fish or meat stock into a thick paste. It is usually served with raw onion and mojo.
Queso asado con mojo
Grilled local goat’s cheese served with red and green mojo. It is a common starter across the island.
Vieja
Parrotfish, a favourite local fish, usually served whole and grilled or boiled with potatoes. Fish restaurants in harbour villages sell it by weight.

What to drink

Barraquito
A layered Tenerife coffee made with condensed milk, a liqueur, espresso, frothed milk, lemon peel and cinnamon. Ask for it “sin licor” if you want it without alcohol.
Tenerife wine
Tenerife has several wine denominations of origin, and its vines grow on volcanic soil. Listán negro reds and listán blanco whites are the local staples.
Ron miel
Honey rum, a sweet Canarian liqueur often offered as a free shot at the end of a meal.

Culture and etiquette

Tenerife is part of Spain, but Canarians have their own accent, food and traditions, shaped by links with Latin America. Life in resorts runs on holiday time; outside them, people keep Spanish hours.

  • Lunch is usually from about 1.30 to 4 pm and dinner from 8 pm, although resort restaurants serve all day.
  • Buses are called guaguas in the Canary Islands, and you will see the word on timetables and stops.
  • Cover up with a shirt and shoes when you leave the beach for shops, cafés and churches.
  • Carnival in Santa Cruz de Tenerife, usually in February, is one of the world’s biggest street carnivals, and hotels in the north fill up.
  • A few words of Spanish such as “buenos días” and “gracias” are appreciated, especially in villages.

Getting to and around Tenerife

Airports

  • TFSTenerife South Airport (Tenerife Sur)Most UK flights land here. Transfers take about 20 minutes to Los Cristianos, Playa de las Américas and Costa Adeje, about 35 minutes to Los Gigantes and about 1 hour to Puerto de la Cruz.
  • TFNTenerife North Airport (Tenerife Norte–Ciudad de La Laguna)Mainly flights from mainland Spain and the other islands, with few from the UK. It is about 15 minutes from Santa Cruz and La Laguna and about 30 minutes from Puerto de la Cruz.

Getting around

Buses
TITSA buses link the airports, resorts and main towns, with good routes along the motorways. A rechargeable ten+ travel card is cheaper than paying cash each trip.
Car rental
A hire car is the easiest way to see Teide, Masca and the north. Spain drives on the right; mountain roads are narrow with hairpin bends, and Teide can be closed by snow in winter.
Tram
The Tenerife tram runs between Santa Cruz de Tenerife and La Laguna, which is handy for a day in the island’s two historic cities.
Ferries
Ferries leave Los Cristianos for La Gomera, a crossing of about 1 hour, and Santa Cruz for Gran Canaria. A day trip to La Gomera is easy from the south.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Spain, including the Canary Islands, for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must be less than 10 years old on arrival and valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System may ask you to give fingerprints and a photo at the border. EU citizens need a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    The currency is the euro and cards are widely accepted. The Canary Islands charge IGIC, a sales tax lower than Spanish VAT, so some goods are cheaper than in mainland Spain.

  • Plugs and power

    Sockets are type C and F (two round pins) at 230 V, so UK travellers need an adapter.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is not expected. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good table service is appreciated.

  • Safety

    Tenerife is generally safe; watch for pickpockets in busy resorts and never leave valuables in a car. The Atlantic can be rough, so swim at lifeguarded beaches and follow the flags.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a GHIC or EHIC for state healthcare, plus travel insurance. The sun is strong all year, and the altitude at the top of the Teide cable car can affect people with heart or breathing conditions.

  • Drinking water

    Tap water is treated and safe to drink in most places, but much of it is desalinated and tastes of minerals or chlorine. Most visitors and locals drink bottled water.

  • Accessibility

    The southern resorts have long, mostly flat promenades and several beaches with ramps and assisted bathing in season. Old towns such as La Orotava are steep and cobbled, and mountain trails are not accessible.

Tenerife itineraries

3 days

Tenerife in 3 days

A short break that combines the beach, Mount Teide and the island’s historic north.

  1. Day 1Beach and promenade in the southSettle into Costa Adeje or Los Cristianos and spend the day on the beach. In the evening, walk the seafront promenade towards Playa de las Américas for sunset.
  2. Day 2Teide National ParkDrive or take a tour up to Teide National Park early, before the cable car queues build. Walk the short Roques de García loop, then come down through the pine forest to Vilaflor.
  3. Day 3La Orotava and GarachicoHead north to the balconied streets of La Orotava, then on to the dragon tree at Icod de los Vinos and the rock pools of Garachico. Return south along the motorway for dinner.
7 days

Tenerife in 7 days

A week that covers the mountains, both coasts and a day trip to La Gomera.

  1. Day 1Arrive and unwindCheck in on the south coast and spend the afternoon by the pool or on the beach. Keep the first evening simple with a stroll along the promenade.
  2. Day 2Teide National ParkTake the cable car up Mount Teide or walk the Roques de García loop. If you have a summit permit, plan the day around your time slot.
  3. Day 3Whales and Los GigantesJoin a morning whale-watching trip from the south-west coast. In the afternoon, visit Los Gigantes for the cliffs and a swim.
  4. Day 4Masca and TenoDrive the mountain road to Masca, or walk the gorge if you have booked. Continue to Punta de Teno for the lighthouse, checking access rules first.
  5. Day 5The north coastVisit La Orotava, Puerto de la Cruz and Garachico. Stop at a guachinche or a village restaurant for papas arrugadas and local wine.
  6. Day 6La Laguna and AnagaWalk the old streets of La Laguna in the morning, then drive into the Anaga mountains for a forest trail. Finish at Playa de las Teresitas.
  7. Day 7La GomeraTake the ferry from Los Cristianos to San Sebastián de La Gomera for a day of green valleys and quiet villages. Be back for a final dinner by the sea.
