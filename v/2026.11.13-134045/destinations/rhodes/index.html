About Rhodes

Rhodes is the largest island of the Dodecanese, in the south-eastern Aegean about 18 km from the Turkish coast. It is one of the sunniest places in Greece, with a season that runs from April to October. The capital, Rhodes Town, sits at the northern tip of the island, and most resorts lie along the east coast, which faces the morning sun and calmer water.

History is everywhere. The Knights of St John ruled Rhodes from 1309 to 1522 and built the walled Old Town, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Before them came ancient Greek cities at Lindos, Kamiros and Ialysos; after them the Ottomans and, from 1912, the Italians. Rhodes joined Greece with the rest of the Dodecanese in 1947–1948.

The east coast has the long sandy and pebble beaches, from Faliraki to Lindos and beyond. The west coast is windier, popular with windsurfers, and greener inland, with pine-covered hills, small villages and the island’s wine country around Embonas. The far south, around Prasonisi, is wilder and much quieter, with long empty beaches and few big hotels.

When to go to Rhodes

The best time to visit Rhodes is May–June or September–October, when it is warm and sunny without the peak heat and prices. July and August are hottest, at around 31°C, and busiest. Winter is mild and green but wet, and many resort hotels close.

Best things to do in Rhodes

  1. 011. Explore the Old Town of Rhodes

    The walled Old Town of Rhodes is one of the best-preserved medieval towns in Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Walk the Street of the Knights to the Palace of the Grand Master, then lose yourself in the lanes of the old market quarter. The Archaeological Museum is in the Knights’ former hospital.

  2. 022. Climb the acropolis of Lindos

    Lindos, about 50 km south of Rhodes Town, is a white village below a rock topped by an ancient acropolis and the Temple of Athena Lindia. The walk up is steep but short. Go early in summer, before the heat and the coach tours.

  3. 033. Swim at St Paul’s Bay

    St Paul’s Bay is a small, almost enclosed cove just below Lindos acropolis, with clear, calm water. Tradition says the apostle Paul landed here. It is busy in summer, so arrive early. There are sunbeds and a small chapel by the water.

  4. 044. Visit the Valley of the Butterflies

    Petaloudes, the Valley of the Butterflies, is a shaded, wooded gorge in the west of Rhodes, about 25 km from Rhodes Town, with streams and wooden bridges. From about June to September it fills with Jersey tiger moths. Walk quietly and do not disturb them.

  5. 055. Take a day trip to Symi

    The small island of Symi is about an hour to an hour and a half by boat from Rhodes Town. Its harbour, Gialos, is ringed by colourful neoclassical houses. Most trips also stop at the Monastery of Archangel Michael Panormitis.

  6. 066. See Ancient Kamiros

    Kamiros, on the west coast of Rhodes, is the excavated ruin of one of the island’s three ancient cities. Streets, houses, a temple and a water cistern climb a hillside above the sea. It is quieter than Lindos and makes a good stop on a west-coast drive.

  7. 077. Walk up to Filerimos

    Filerimos is a pine-covered hill above Ialysos, about 15 km from Rhodes Town. It has a restored monastery, the remains of an ancient temple and a path lined with the Stations of the Cross. Peacocks roam the grounds and the views reach the coast.

  8. 088. Relax at Kallithea Springs

    Kallithea Springs is a restored 1920s spa on the coast south of Rhodes Town, built in Italian times with domes, colonnades and pebble mosaics. Rocky coves around it are good for snorkelling. There is an entrance fee.

Hidden gems

  • Monolithos CastleMonolithos is a ruined castle of the Knights perched on a sheer rock above the west coast of Rhodes. A small chapel stands inside the walls, and the sunsets over the sea are some of the island’s best.
  • Seven SpringsEpta Piges, the Seven Springs, is a shaded valley inland from Kolymbia where springs feed a small lake. You can walk to it through a narrow, ankle-deep water tunnel, or take the path above.
  • PrasonisiPrasonisi, at the southern tip of Rhodes, is a sandbar joining the island to a small islet, with the Aegean on one side and the Mediterranean on the other. Steady winds make it a well-known spot for windsurfing and kitesurfing.

Where to stay in Rhodes

  • Rhodes Town

    Good for: CultureNightlifeFirst-timers

    Rhodes Town combines the medieval Old Town with a modern new town, beaches and Mandraki harbour. It has the best choice of restaurants, bars and museums, and boats to Symi. Stays inside the Old Town are mostly small hotels.

  • Lindos

    Good for: CouplesCultureLuxury

    Lindos is a car-free village of white houses below its acropolis, with beaches in its own bay and St Paul’s Bay next door. It is beautiful in the evening once the day-trippers leave. Lanes are steep and stepped.

  • Kolymbia

    Good for: FamiliesQuiet staysBeaches

    Kolymbia is a spread-out resort on the east coast with a long, flat promenade and pebbly beaches, close to Tsambika beach and Seven Springs. It is calmer than Faliraki and popular with families.

  • Ixia and Ialysos

    Good for: BudgetFirst-timers

    Ixia and Ialysos are on the windier west coast, close to Rhodes Town and the airport, with shingle beaches and big hotels. They suit windsurfers and anyone who wants easy access to the town.

  • Faliraki

    Good for: NightlifeFamiliesBeaches

    Faliraki is a large resort on the east coast, about 15 km south of Rhodes Town, with a long sandy beach and a water park. It is known for nightlife but also has plenty of family hotels.

Food and drink in Rhodes

Rhodes cooks with the same olive oil, herbs and seafood as the rest of Greece, plus its own village dishes. Tavernas in the south and in mountain villages are the best places to find them.

Dishes to try

Pitaroudia
Fritters of chickpeas or tomato with onion and herbs, served as a meze. They are one of the island’s best-known dishes.
Matsi
Handmade Rhodian pasta, rolled thin and cut into small pieces, usually served with butter and grated cheese.
Giaprakia
The Rhodian name for vine leaves stuffed with rice, herbs and often minced meat.
Symi shrimp
Tiny red shrimp from the neighbouring island of Symi, fried whole and eaten shell and all.
Melekouni
A bar of sesame seeds and honey, flavoured with orange and spices, traditionally handed out at Rhodes weddings and christenings.

What to drink

Souma
A clear grape spirit distilled on Rhodes, especially around Embonas, served in small glasses with meze.
Rhodes wine
Rhodes has its own protected wine, made mainly from the white Athiri and red Mandilaria grapes grown on the slopes of Mount Attavyros.
Greek coffee
Strong coffee boiled in a small pot and served unfiltered; let the grounds settle before you drink.

Culture and etiquette

Rhodes is Greek Orthodox, with a strong sense of its own long history under the Knights, the Ottomans and the Italians. Family, food and religious festivals shape local life.

  • Cover your shoulders and knees in churches and monasteries, including at Filerimos and Tsambika.
  • Walk up to Lindos acropolis rather than riding a donkey.
  • Lunch is late and dinner later; many Rhodians eat around 9 pm.
  • Greek Easter is the biggest celebration of the year and often falls on a different date from Easter in the UK.
  • Avoid the open-palm, fingers-spread gesture known as the moutza, which is an insult in Greece.

Getting to and around Rhodes

Airports

  • RHORhodes International Airport “Diagoras”On the north-west coast at Paradeisi, about 15 km from Rhodes Town. Transfers take about 25 minutes to Rhodes Town or Faliraki, 40 minutes to Kolymbia and 1 hour to Lindos.

Getting around

Buses
Public buses run from Rhodes Town to the east-coast resorts, Lindos, the airport and the west coast. East-coast buses leave from near the New Market; services are frequent in summer and sparse in winter.
Car rental
Driving is on the right. A car is the easiest way to see the quieter south and west of Rhodes; main roads are good, but village lanes are narrow and parking near the Old Town is limited.
Taxis
Taxis wait at the airport and at the main rank in Rhodes Town. Fares to fixed destinations are set; agree or check the price before you set off.
Boats
Ferries and day boats leave Rhodes Town for Symi and other Dodecanese islands. In summer, small boats also link east-coast resorts such as Faliraki and Lindos.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Greece for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before you arrive and be valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System records non-EU travellers’ fingerprints and photo at the border, so allow extra time. EU citizens can travel with a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    Greece uses the euro. Cards are widely accepted in Rhodes Town and the resorts, but carry some cash for villages, buses and small tavernas. Hotels also charge a nightly climate resilience fee set by the Greek government, paid locally.

  • Plugs

    Greece uses type C and F two-pin plugs at 230 V, 50 Hz. UK travellers need a plug adaptor.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is not compulsory. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service in a restaurant is normal, in cash if possible.

  • Safety

    Rhodes has low crime, but watch your belongings in busy areas. Wildfires are a risk across Greece in summer: follow local advice and emergency alerts, and never light fires outdoors. The emergency number is 112.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a GHIC (or a valid EHIC), which covers state healthcare in Greece, and also take out travel insurance with medical cover. The summer sun is strong: use high-factor sun cream and drink plenty of water.

  • Water

    Tap water on Rhodes is treated and generally considered safe, but taste and quality vary from place to place and most visitors drink bottled water. Ask at your hotel.

  • Accessibility

    The Old Town of Rhodes is paved with uneven cobbles and pebbles, and Lindos is steep and stepped, with no step-free route up to the acropolis. Modern resorts such as Faliraki and Kolymbia are flatter, with level promenades. Ask your hotel about access before booking.
