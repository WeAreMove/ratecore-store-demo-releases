About Malta

Malta is an archipelago of three inhabited islands, Malta, Gozo and Comino, about 80 km south of Sicily in the central Mediterranean. It packs a remarkable amount into a small space: a Baroque capital, a walled medieval city, harbours lined with forts and prehistoric temples that are older than the Egyptian pyramids.

Valletta, the capital, was built by the Knights of St John after the Great Siege of 1565 and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Gozo, a short ferry ride away, is greener and quieter, while tiny Comino is known for the Blue Lagoon. Beaches are mostly small and rocky, with a few sandy bays in the north.

Malta is easy for UK visitors: English is an official language, cars drive on the left and plugs are the same as at home. Summers are hot and dry, the sea is warm from June to November, and winters are mild and fairly sunny. The currency is the euro, and the flight from London takes just over three hours.

At a glance

  • Malta is an island country in the central Mediterranean, about 80 km south of Sicily, made up of the islands of Malta, Gozo and Comino.
  • Malta’s capital, Valletta, was built by the Knights of St John after the Great Siege of 1565 and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  • Malta has two official languages, Maltese and English, and uses UK-style three-pin plugs.

When to go to Malta

The best time to visit Malta is April to June and September to October, when it is warm and sunny without the peak summer heat. July and August are hot, with highs around 32°C, and the busiest months. Winter is mild and good for sightseeing, but the sea is too cool for most swimmers.

Best things to do in Malta

  1. 011. Explore Valletta, the fortified capital

    Valletta is a small, grid-planned city on a peninsula between two harbours, built by the Knights of St John from 1566. Walk Republic Street to the Grand Master’s Palace, then go to the Upper Barrakka Gardens, where the Saluting Battery fires its guns at noon and 4pm.

  2. 022. See Caravaggio at St John’s Co-Cathedral

    St John’s Co-Cathedral in Valletta has a plain exterior and one of Europe’s richest Baroque interiors, with a floor of marble tombstones of the knights. Its oratory holds Caravaggio’s The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist, painted in 1608, the only work he signed. Cover shoulders and knees.

  3. 033. Walk the silent city of Mdina

    Mdina is Malta’s walled former capital, on a hilltop in the centre of the island, with quiet lanes of palaces and churches and views across the whole island from its bastions. Next door, Rabat has the early Christian St Paul’s Catacombs and good bakeries for pastizzi.

  4. 044. Spend a day on Gozo

    Gozo, Malta’s greener sister island, is about 25 minutes by ferry from Ċirkewwa or about 45 minutes by fast ferry from Valletta. See the Ċittadella in Victoria and the Ġgantija temples, which date from about 3600–3200 BC, then swim in a rocky bay or walk the cliffs.

  5. 055. Swim in Comino’s Blue Lagoon

    The Blue Lagoon is a channel of shallow, turquoise water between Comino and the islet of Cominotto. In season, visitors need a free time slot booked online before landing, and boats run from Ċirkewwa, Mġarr and Sliema. Go early or late, as the middle of the day is very busy.

  6. 066. Go underground at the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum

    The Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum in Paola is a prehistoric underground burial complex carved into the rock, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visits are in small groups to protect it, so tickets sell out weeks ahead. Photography is not allowed inside.

  7. 077. Cross the Grand Harbour to the Three Cities

    Birgu, Senglea and Cospicua, known as the Three Cities, face Valletta across the Grand Harbour and were the knights’ first base. Cross by ferry or in a traditional dgħajsa water taxi, walk the waterfront at Birgu and visit Fort St Angelo.

  8. 088. Take a boat into the Blue Grotto

    The Blue Grotto is a group of sea caves on Malta’s south coast, reached by small boats from Wied iż-Żurrieq when the sea is calm. Go in the morning for the brightest colours, then visit the nearby temples of Ħaġar Qim and Mnajdra on the clifftop.

Hidden gems

  • Wied il-Għasri, GozoWied il-Għasri is a narrow, steep-sided inlet on Gozo’s north coast that ends in a tiny pebble beach. Steps cut into the rock lead down, and the water is clear and good for snorkelling on calm days.
  • Dingli CliffsThe Dingli Cliffs on Malta’s south-west coast rise about 250 m above the sea, near the islands’ highest point. A quiet road and footpaths run along the top, with views out to the islet of Filfla, and sunset here is a local favourite.
  • Għar DalamGħar Dalam, near Birżebbuġa, is a cave where bones of dwarf elephants and hippos from the Ice Age were found, along with the earliest evidence of people on Malta. A small museum shows the finds.

Where to stay in Malta

  • Sliema

    Good for: First-timersFood loversBudget

    Sliema, across Marsamxett Harbour from Valletta, has a long seafront promenade, rocky swimming spots and plenty of hotels and shops. The ferry to Valletta takes about 10 minutes, and boat trips leave from the Strand.

  • St Julian’s

    Good for: NightlifeFirst-timers

    St Julian’s, next to Sliema, is Malta’s nightlife hub, with the bars and clubs of Paceville and a small sandy beach at St George’s Bay. Spinola Bay, with its fishing boats, is quieter.

  • Mellieħa

    Good for: FamiliesBeachesQuiet stays

    Mellieħa, in the north of Malta, sits on a ridge above Għadira Bay, the islands’ largest sandy beach. It is quieter than the central resorts and close to the ferries for Gozo and Comino.

  • Valletta

    Good for: CultureCouplesFood lovers

    Valletta suits people who want history, restaurants and evening walks on the doorstep, in boutique hotels and apartments in old palazzi. It is compact and car-free in places, but streets are steep and there are no sandy beaches.

  • Gozo

    Good for: Quiet staysWalkersCouples

    Gozo is the choice for a slower stay, in farmhouses with pools, small hotels in Xlendi or Marsalforn or in Victoria. It is greener and quieter than Malta, with good walking and diving.

Food and drink in Malta

Maltese food mixes Sicilian, North African and British influences, with rabbit, fresh fish and bread at its heart. Pastry snacks from village bakeries are part of daily life.

Dishes to try

Pastizzi
Flaky diamond-shaped pastries filled with ricotta or mushy peas, sold hot from bakeries and pastizzerias for very little.
Stuffat tal-fenek
Rabbit slow-cooked in red wine, tomato and garlic, often seen as Malta’s national dish and served with spaghetti or chips.
Ħobż biż-żejt
Maltese sourdough bread rubbed with tomato and topped with olive oil, tuna, capers, olives and onion.
Ftira
A ring-shaped Maltese flatbread, either filled like a sandwich or, on Gozo, baked like a pizza with potato, anchovies or cheese.
Torta tal-lampuki
A fish pie made with lampuki (dorado or mahi-mahi), spinach, olives and capers, eaten in autumn when the fish are in season.
Imqaret
Deep-fried pastries filled with dates and flavoured with aniseed, sold from stalls at festas and markets.

What to drink

Kinnie
A bittersweet Maltese soft drink made with bitter oranges and herbs, on sale everywhere since the 1950s.
Cisk
Malta’s best-known lager, brewed on the island and served cold in every bar.
Maltese wine
Small producers on Malta and Gozo make wines from local grapes such as Għirgentina (white) and Ġellewża (red).

Culture and etiquette

Malta is a Catholic country with deep traditions, and village life still turns around the parish church. People are friendly and almost everyone speaks English.

  • Cover your shoulders and knees in churches, including St John’s Co-Cathedral.
  • Swimwear is for the beach: cover up when you walk into towns, shops and restaurants.
  • Each village celebrates its patron saint with a summer festa of processions, band marches and fireworks, and visitors are welcome.
  • A few words of Maltese, such as bonġu (good morning) and grazzi (thank you), are always appreciated.
  • Sunday is a family day, and some smaller shops close or keep short hours.

Getting to and around Malta

Airports

  • MLAMalta International AirportMalta International Airport, at Luqa, is the only airport and is about 6 km south-west of Valletta. By road it takes about 15–20 minutes to Valletta, 25–30 minutes to Sliema and St Julian’s, about 35 minutes to Buġibba and about 45 minutes to Mellieħa. Allow about 50 minutes to the Gozo ferry at Ċirkewwa.

Getting around

Buses
Malta Public Transport runs buses across Malta and Gozo, including express routes from the airport. A Tallinja card makes repeat journeys cheaper, but buses can be slow and crowded in summer.
Ferries
Small ferries link Valletta with Sliema and with the Three Cities in a few minutes. The Gozo Channel ferry from Ċirkewwa to Mġarr takes about 25 minutes and runs day and night, and a fast ferry links Valletta and Gozo in about 45 minutes.
Car rental
Malta drives on the left, like the UK, and distances are short. Traffic is heavy around the towns and parking in Valletta is limited, so a car is most useful for Gozo and the countryside.
Taxis and ride-hailing
White taxis and ride-hailing apps operate across Malta. Agree the fare or use an app with a fixed price, especially from the airport.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    Malta is in the EU and the Schengen area. UK passport holders can visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa, and your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before you arrive and be valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System records non-EU travellers’ details at the border. EU citizens can travel with a passport or national ID card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    Malta uses the euro (EUR). Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for bakeries, kiosks and village festas.

  • Plugs and power

    Malta uses type G plugs, the same three-pin plugs as the UK, at 230 V and 50 Hz. UK visitors do not need an adaptor.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is appreciated but not expected. Around 5–10% in restaurants is usual if service is not included, and rounding up taxi fares is fine.

  • Safety

    Malta has low crime and is generally very safe. Watch for pickpockets in busy areas and around the bars of Paceville at night. Cliff edges are often unfenced, and the summer sun is strong.

  • Health

    UK travellers can use a GHIC for state healthcare in Malta, including Mater Dei Hospital and health centres, but you should still take out travel insurance. Use high-factor sun cream and drink plenty of water in summer.

  • Drinking water

    Tap water in Malta is safe to drink. Much of it comes from desalination, so it can taste salty or of chlorine, and most people drink bottled water.

  • Accessibility

    Valletta’s streets are steep, with many stepped pavements, though the Barrakka Lift links the Grand Harbour waterfront with the Upper Barrakka Gardens. Most buses are low-floor with ramps. Historic sites and rocky beaches can be hard to reach with a wheelchair, so check access before you go.

3 days

Malta in 3 days

A short break that covers Valletta, Mdina and the Grand Harbour.

  1. Day 1VallettaStart at St John’s Co-Cathedral, then walk to the Upper Barrakka Gardens for the noon gun. Spend the afternoon in the streets and museums of Valletta and have dinner on the harbour side.
  2. Day 2Mdina, Rabat and Dingli CliffsWalk the lanes and bastions of Mdina, then see St Paul’s Catacombs in Rabat. Finish with sunset at the Dingli Cliffs.
  3. Day 3The Three CitiesCross the Grand Harbour by ferry or dgħajsa to Birgu and visit Fort St Angelo. If you have booked ahead, add the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum in nearby Paola.
7 days

Malta in 7 days

A week that adds Gozo, Comino and the south coast to the highlights of Malta.

  1. Day 1VallettaSee St John’s Co-Cathedral, the Grand Master’s Palace and the Upper Barrakka Gardens. Take the ferry to Sliema for an evening walk along the seafront.
  2. Day 2The Three Cities and the HypogeumCross to Birgu and Fort St Angelo in the morning. Visit the Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum in the afternoon if you have tickets.
  3. Day 3Mdina, Rabat and DingliSpend the day in Mdina and Rabat, then drive or take the bus to the Dingli Cliffs for sunset.
  4. Day 4The south coastTake a boat into the Blue Grotto in the morning and walk round the temples of Ħaġar Qim and Mnajdra. End in Marsaxlokk for fish by the harbour, or go on a Sunday morning for its market.
  5. Day 5Comino and the Blue LagoonBook your Blue Lagoon slot, take an early boat from Ċirkewwa and swim before the crowds arrive. Walk across Comino to its watchtower.
  6. Day 6GozoTake the ferry to Gozo and see the Ċittadella in Victoria and the Ġgantija temples. Finish with a swim at Wied il-Għasri or Xlendi.
  7. Day 7Golden BaySpend a last lazy day on the sand at Golden Bay or Għajn Tuffieħa in the north-west. Watch the sunset over the sea.
