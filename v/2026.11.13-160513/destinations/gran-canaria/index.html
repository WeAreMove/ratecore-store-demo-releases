About Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria is the third-largest of Spain’s Canary Islands and the second most populous, with about 860,000 people. It lies in the Atlantic off the north-west coast of Africa, between Tenerife and Fuerteventura, and a direct flight from London takes about 4 hours 20 minutes. Like the rest of the Canary Islands, it is part of Spain and keeps the same time as the UK all year.

The island is almost circular, about 50 km across, and rises to nearly 2,000 m at its centre. Its sunny south has the big resorts of Playa del Inglés, Maspalomas, Puerto Rico and Puerto de Mogán, with long beaches and a huge field of sand dunes. The greener north is home to Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, one of Spain’s largest cities.

Away from the coast, the mountains are full of deep ravines, pine forest, cave houses and quiet villages such as Tejeda and Teror. Almost half the island is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, and the ancient cave sites around Risco Caído are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. You can mix a beach week with mountain walks, city food and history.

When to go to Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria has beach weather all year, with winter highs around 21–22 °C and summer highs around 27–28 °C. March–June and September–November are the best all-round months, warm and quieter than the school holidays. Winter is the high season for winter-sun visitors.

Best things to do in Gran Canaria

  1. 011. Walk across the Maspalomas Dunes

    The Maspalomas Dunes are a protected nature reserve of sand dunes of about 400 hectares between Playa del Inglés and Maspalomas, in the south of Gran Canaria. Marked paths cross them to the sea, and the walk to the Maspalomas lighthouse along the beach takes an hour or more. Stay on the paths and take water and shade.

  2. 022. Climb to Roque Nublo

    Roque Nublo is a volcanic rock column about 80 m tall near the summit of Gran Canaria and the island’s best-known landmark. A path of about 1.5 km each way leads up from La Goleta, where private parking has been removed in favour of shuttle buses. Visits must be booked in advance through the Cabildo de Gran Canaria.

  3. 033. Explore Vegueta in Las Palmas

    Vegueta is the old quarter of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, founded in 1478, with cobbled lanes, the Cathedral of Santa Ana and colonial houses. The Casa de Colón museum recalls Christopher Columbus, who stopped on the island in 1492.

  4. 044. Swim at Las Canteras

    Las Canteras is a city beach of golden sand more than 3 km long in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. A natural reef offshore, La Barra, keeps much of the water calm and good for snorkelling. A long promenade runs its full length, with cafés and restaurants.

  5. 055. Visit Tejeda and Pico de las Nieves

    Tejeda is a mountain village in the centre of Gran Canaria, known for its almond sweets and views of Roque Nublo and Roque Bentayga. Nearby, Pico de las Nieves at 1,949 m has a viewpoint over the island and, on clear days, Mount Teide on Tenerife. Roads are steep and winding, so allow time.

  6. 066. Spend an evening in Puerto de Mogán

    Puerto de Mogán is a small harbour on the south-west coast of Gran Canaria with low white houses, flower-covered bridges and canals. It has a sheltered beach and a street market on Fridays. Come in the late afternoon, when day visitors leave and the harbour quietens.

  7. 077. See the Cueva Pintada in Gáldar

    The Cueva Pintada Museum and Archaeological Park in Gáldar protects a cave painted with geometric patterns by the island’s pre-Hispanic people. Walkways lead over the excavated remains of their village. It is about 30 minutes’ drive west of Las Palmas.

  8. 088. Swim in Agaete’s natural pools

    Agaete, on the north-west coast of Gran Canaria, has natural seawater pools at Las Salinas and a small port at Puerto de las Nieves. Inland, the Agaete valley grows coffee, oranges and grapes. Ferries to Tenerife also leave from here.

Hidden gems

  • Barranco de GuayadequeThe Guayadeque ravine, between Agüimes and Ingenio in the east of Gran Canaria, is a deep valley with cave houses, a small cave chapel and cave restaurants. A road runs up the valley floor and walking paths continue higher.
  • TerorTeror is a hill town in the north of Gran Canaria with some of the island’s finest wooden balconies. Its Basilica of Nuestra Señora del Pino honours the island’s patron saint, and a market fills the square on Sundays.
  • Artenara and Risco CaídoArtenara is the highest village on Gran Canaria, at about 1,200 m, with homes and a chapel carved into the rock. It is the gateway to the Risco Caído and Sacred Mountains landscape, listed by UNESCO in 2019 for its ancient cave sites and pre-Hispanic heritage.

Where to stay in Gran Canaria

  • Maspalomas and Meloneras

    Good for: CouplesLuxuryBeaches

    Maspalomas and neighbouring Meloneras sit at the southern tip of the island around the lighthouse, with a seafront promenade, golf courses and larger resort hotels. They are calmer and more upmarket than Playa del Inglés.

  • Puerto Rico and Amadores

    Good for: FamiliesBeaches

    Puerto Rico and Amadores are purpose-built resorts in a sunny bay on the south-west coast, with sheltered beaches and calm water. Hillside apartments mean some steep walks, but both beaches suit young children.

  • Las Palmas de Gran Canaria

    Good for: CultureFood loversSurfers

    Las Palmas is a real Spanish city with a city beach, Las Canteras, a historic quarter, museums and good tapas. It is cloudier than the south but has the island’s best food scene and surf at La Cícer.

  • Playa del Inglés

    Good for: NightlifeBudgetBeaches

    Playa del Inglés is Gran Canaria’s largest resort, next to the Maspalomas Dunes, with a long beach, many apartments and the island’s busiest nightlife. It is also one of Europe’s best-known LGBTQ+ holiday spots.

  • Puerto de Mogán

    Good for: CouplesQuiet stays

    Puerto de Mogán is a small, low-rise harbour resort with canals, a beach and fish restaurants. It is the quietest of the south-coast resorts and especially pleasant in the evening.

Food and drink in Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria’s cooking is classic Canarian: potatoes with mojo, fresh fish, stews, local cheese and gofio. The island also makes its own rum, wine and even coffee.

Dishes to try

Papas arrugadas con mojo
Small potatoes boiled in their skins in very salty water, served with red and green mojo sauces. They come with almost every meal.
Sancocho canario
Salted fish, usually wreckfish (cherne), boiled with potatoes and sweet potato and served with mojo and a ball of gofio. It is a traditional dish for Easter and family meals.
Queso de flor de Guía
A protected cheese from the north of the island, made mainly from sheep’s milk, curdled with the flowers of the wild cardoon. It has a soft, creamy texture and a slightly bitter edge.
Ropa vieja
A stew of chickpeas, potatoes and shredded meat, usually beef or chicken, flavoured with garlic and paprika. The name means “old clothes”.
Pata asada
Slow-roasted leg of pork, carved thin and served in rolls or on plates. It is a favourite snack in Las Palmas.
Bienmesabe
A sweet almond cream made with ground almonds, sugar, egg yolks and lemon, linked above all with Tejeda. It is eaten on its own or with ice cream.

What to drink

Rum from Arucas
The town of Arucas, in the north of Gran Canaria, has made rum from sugar cane for more than a century. Ron miel, a sweet honey rum, is often offered after a meal.
Agaete coffee and wine
Coffee is grown in the Agaete valley, one of the few places in Europe where it is farmed. The valley and the island’s volcanic hills also produce wines under the Gran Canaria designation of origin.

Culture and etiquette

Gran Canaria mixes busy international resorts with traditional Canarian towns and a big, lively capital. Away from the south, everyday life follows Spanish hours.

  • Lunch is usually from about 1.30 to 4 pm and dinner from 8 pm, although resort restaurants serve all day.
  • Buses are called guaguas in the Canary Islands, and the yellow city buses in Las Palmas are separate from Global, the island bus company.
  • Carnival in Las Palmas and Maspalomas, in February and March, brings parades, costumes and late nights.
  • Nudism is accepted on parts of the Maspalomas beach, but cover up away from the sand.
  • A few words of Spanish such as “buenos días” and “gracias” go a long way, especially in mountain villages.

Getting to and around Gran Canaria

Airports

  • LPAGran Canaria Airport (Gando)The island’s only airport is on the east coast between Telde and Ingenio. Transfers take about 25 minutes to Playa del Inglés and Las Palmas, about 30 minutes to Maspalomas and Meloneras, about 35 minutes to Puerto Rico and about 45 minutes to Puerto de Mogán.

Getting around

Buses
Global runs the island buses between the airport, Las Palmas and the southern resorts, with frequent services on the GC-1 motorway. In Las Palmas, the yellow city buses cover the old town, beaches and port.
Car rental
A car is the best way to see the mountains and the north. Spain drives on the right; mountain roads are narrow and twisting, and parking near Roque Nublo is restricted.
Taxis
Taxis are metered and easy to find in resorts and Las Palmas. They are a good option for short trips and airport transfers.
Ferries
Ferries link Agaete with Santa Cruz de Tenerife in about 1 hour 20 minutes, and Las Palmas with Fuerteventura and Lanzarote.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Spain, including the Canary Islands, for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must be less than 10 years old on arrival and valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System may ask you to give fingerprints and a photo at the border. EU citizens need a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    The currency is the euro and cards are widely accepted. The Canary Islands charge IGIC, a sales tax lower than Spanish VAT, so some goods are cheaper than in mainland Spain.

  • Plugs and power

    Sockets are type C and F (two round pins) at 230 V, so UK travellers need an adapter.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is not expected. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good table service is appreciated.

  • Safety

    Gran Canaria is generally safe; watch for pickpockets in busy resorts and markets and never leave valuables in a car. Atlantic waves and currents can be strong, so swim at lifeguarded beaches and follow the flags.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a GHIC or EHIC for state healthcare, plus travel insurance. The sun is strong all year, even when it is breezy, so use high-factor sun cream.

  • Drinking water

    Tap water is treated and safe to drink in most places, but much of it is desalinated and tastes of minerals or chlorine. Most visitors and locals drink bottled water.

  • Accessibility

    Las Canteras and the main southern resorts have flat promenades, and several beaches have ramps and assisted bathing points in season. Vegueta is cobbled, hillside resorts such as Puerto Rico are steep, and mountain trails are not accessible.
