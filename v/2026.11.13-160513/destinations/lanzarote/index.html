About Lanzarote

Lanzarote is one of Spain’s Canary Islands, about 125 km off the coast of Africa and around 4 hours by air from London. It is about 60 km long and 25 km wide, with around 167,000 residents, and the whole island has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 1993. Its capital and main port is Arrecife, on the east coast.

Eruptions between 1730 and 1736 covered a quarter of the island in lava, and that landscape is now protected in Timanfaya National Park. Elsewhere, farmers grow vines in hollows dug into black volcanic ash, and low white houses with green or blue shutters dot the plains. Rain is rare, and steady trade winds keep the island breezy for much of the year.

Much of the island’s look is down to the artist César Manrique, who turned caves, lava tubes and viewpoints into public spaces and campaigned against high-rise building. Most visitors stay in the sunny resorts of Puerto del Carmen, Playa Blanca or Costa Teguise, with Papagayo’s sandy coves, Manrique’s sites and the island of La Graciosa all within easy reach.

When to go to Lanzarote

Lanzarote is warm and dry all year, with winter highs around 21–22 °C and summer highs around 28–29 °C. March–June and September–November are the best all-round months, with warm days and fewer crowds. Summer is the hottest and windiest time, and also the busiest.

Best things to do in Lanzarote

  1. 011. Tour Timanfaya National Park

    Timanfaya National Park, in the south-west of Lanzarote, protects a landscape of volcanoes and lava created by the eruptions of 1730–1736. The ticket for the Montañas del Fuego visitor area includes a coach tour along the Ruta de los Volcanes, which you cannot drive yourself. At Islote de Hilario, staff show how hot the ground is just below the surface.

  2. 022. Visit Jameos del Agua

    Jameos del Agua, in the north of Lanzarote, is a section of volcanic lava tube that César Manrique turned into gardens, a pool and a concert hall. A dark saltwater lagoon inside is home to tiny blind white crabs found almost nowhere else. The nearby Cueva de los Verdes offers guided walks through another part of the same tube.

  3. 033. Taste wine in La Geria

    La Geria is a wine-growing area in the centre of Lanzarote where vines grow in hollows dug into black volcanic ash, each sheltered by a low, curved stone wall. Wineries along the LZ-30 road offer tastings of the local Malvasía volcánica. Go with a driver or a tour if you plan to taste.

  4. 044. Swim at the Papagayo beaches

    The Papagayo beaches are a string of sandy coves on the southern tip of Lanzarote, inside the Los Ajaches natural monument. They are reached by an unpaved track from Playa Blanca, with a small charge per car, or on foot along the coast. There is little shade, so bring water and sun protection.

  5. 055. See César Manrique’s house

    The Fundación César Manrique at Taro de Tahíche is the artist’s former home, built in 1968 over five volcanic bubbles in a lava field. The rooms below ground connect through tunnels in the lava. It also shows his paintings and his work on protecting Lanzarote.

  6. 066. Look out from the Mirador del Río

    The Mirador del Río is a viewpoint built into the cliffs at the northern tip of Lanzarote, designed by César Manrique. Its windows look across a narrow strait to the island of La Graciosa. Clear mornings give the best views.

  7. 077. Take a day trip to La Graciosa

    La Graciosa is a small island off the northern tip of Lanzarote, reached by ferry from Órzola in about 25 minutes. It has sandy tracks instead of paved roads, a harbour village called Caleta de Sebo and quiet beaches. Hire a bike or join a jeep tour to reach the far side.

  8. 088. Visit El Golfo and Los Hervideros

    El Golfo, on the west coast of Lanzarote, is a half-eroded volcanic crater with a bright green lagoon, the Charco de los Clicos, beside a black-sand beach. A short drive south, at Los Hervideros, Atlantic waves surge through lava caves. Late afternoon light is best here.

Hidden gems

  • Jardín de CactusThe Jardín de Cactus at Guatiza, in the north-east of Lanzarote, is César Manrique’s last major work, a garden of cacti set in a former quarry. It sits among fields where cochineal insects are still farmed on prickly pears.
  • Salinas de JanubioThe Salinas de Janubio are traditional salt pans on the south-west coast of Lanzarote, near Playa Blanca. A roadside viewpoint looks over a patchwork of white and coloured pans beside a lagoon and a black-sand beach.
  • HaríaHaría is a green village in the north of Lanzarote, known as the valley of a thousand palms. César Manrique spent his final years here, and his house is open to visitors. A craft market fills the square on Saturday mornings.

Where to stay in Lanzarote

  • Puerto del Carmen

    Good for: First-timersNightlifeBeaches

    Puerto del Carmen is Lanzarote’s biggest resort, with several kilometres of sandy beaches, a long promenade and the most bars and restaurants on the island. Its old harbour is quieter, and the airport is about 10 minutes away.

  • Playa Blanca

    Good for: FamiliesCouplesQuiet stays

    Playa Blanca is a relaxed resort on the southern tip of the island, with a marina, calm beaches and ferries to Fuerteventura. The Papagayo beaches are close by.

  • Arrecife

    Good for: CultureFood loversBudget

    Arrecife is the island’s capital, with a city beach, a seawater lagoon called El Charco de San Ginés and good local tapas bars. Staying here suits travellers who prefer everyday Canarian life to a resort.

  • Famara

    Good for: SurfersWalkersQuiet stays

    Caleta de Famara is a small, low-key village on the north-west coast below the Famara cliffs, beside a long beach known for surfing. Strong currents make the sea dangerous for swimming.

  • Costa Teguise

    Good for: FamiliesBudget

    Costa Teguise is a smaller resort on the east coast, with sheltered beaches, a golf course and good conditions for windsurfing. It is about 15 minutes from the airport and close to Arrecife.

Food and drink in Lanzarote

Lanzarote’s food is simple Canarian cooking: fresh fish, potatoes, goat’s cheese and gofio, with vegetables grown in volcanic soil. Harbour villages such as El Golfo and Playa Quemada are good for grilled fish.

Dishes to try

Papas arrugadas con mojo
Small potatoes boiled in their skins in very salty water, served with red and green mojo sauces. They come with most meals.
Lapas
Limpets grilled in their shells and topped with green mojo or garlic. They are served as a starter in fish restaurants.
Sancocho canario
Salted fish boiled with potatoes and sweet potato, served with mojo and gofio. It is a traditional family dish.
Cabrito
Young goat, usually stewed slowly in a sauce with garlic, herbs and wine. It is a classic of inland restaurants.
Queso de cabra
Local goat’s cheese, eaten fresh, semi-cured or cured, sometimes coated in paprika or gofio. It is often served grilled with mojo.
Potaje de berros
A thick watercress soup with potatoes, pork and corn, often thickened with gofio. It is warming food for cooler evenings.

What to drink

Malvasía volcánica
A dry, aromatic white wine made from Malvasía grapes grown in the volcanic ash of La Geria. It is Lanzarote’s signature wine.
Moscatel
A sweet dessert wine made from Muscat grapes grown on the island. It is served with cheese or sweets.
Barraquito
A layered Canarian coffee with condensed milk, liqueur, espresso, milk, lemon peel and cinnamon. Ask for it “sin licor” for a version without alcohol.

Culture and etiquette

Lanzarote’s identity is tied to its volcanoes and to César Manrique, whose ideas still shape how the island looks. Life outside the resorts is quiet and follows Spanish hours.

  • Lunch is usually from about 1.30 to 4 pm and dinner from 8 pm, although resort restaurants serve all day.
  • Stay on marked paths in Timanfaya and other protected areas; the lava and plants are fragile and removing stones is not allowed.
  • The Sunday market in Teguise, the island’s old capital, is the biggest weekly event and gets very busy by late morning.
  • Buses are called guaguas in the Canary Islands, and you will see the word on timetables and stops.
  • A few words of Spanish such as “buenos días” and “gracias” are appreciated, especially in villages.

Getting to and around Lanzarote

Airports

  • ACECésar Manrique–Lanzarote AirportThe island’s only airport is about 5 km from Arrecife. Transfers take about 10 minutes to Puerto del Carmen and Arrecife, about 15 minutes to Costa Teguise and about 30 minutes to Playa Blanca.

Getting around

Buses
Public buses link the airport, Arrecife and the main resorts, but services to the north, the wine area and the national park are limited.
Car rental
A hire car is the easiest way to see Timanfaya, La Geria and the north, and roads are generally good and quiet. Spain drives on the right; check whether your hire contract covers unpaved tracks such as the Papagayo road.
Taxis
Taxis are metered and easy to find at the airport and in resorts. They are good value for short trips.
Ferries
Ferries cross from Playa Blanca to Corralejo on Fuerteventura in about 30 minutes, and from Órzola to La Graciosa in about 25 minutes.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Spain, including the Canary Islands, for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must be less than 10 years old on arrival and valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System may ask you to give fingerprints and a photo at the border. EU citizens need a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    The currency is the euro and cards are widely accepted. The Canary Islands charge IGIC, a sales tax lower than Spanish VAT, so some goods are cheaper than in mainland Spain.

  • Plugs and power

    Sockets are type C and F (two round pins) at 230 V, so UK travellers need an adapter.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is not expected. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good table service is appreciated.

  • Safety

    Lanzarote is generally safe; take normal care with valuables and never leave them in a car at beaches or viewpoints. Currents are strong on the north and west coasts, so swim at lifeguarded beaches and follow the flags.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a GHIC or EHIC for state healthcare, plus travel insurance. The wind makes the sun feel weaker than it is, so use high-factor sun cream and drink plenty of water.

  • Drinking water

    Almost all of Lanzarote’s tap water comes from desalination plants. It is treated and safe to drink, but it tastes of minerals or chlorine, so most people drink bottled water.

  • Accessibility

    Puerto del Carmen, Playa Blanca and Costa Teguise have long, flat promenades, and some main beaches have ramps and assisted bathing in season. Several César Manrique sites have steps and uneven volcanic floors, and the Cueva de los Verdes tour is not suitable for wheelchairs.

Lanzarote in 3 days

A short break that takes in the volcanoes, Manrique’s best work and the island’s best beaches.

Lanzarote in 7 days

A week that covers the volcanoes, the north, a neighbouring island and plenty of beach time.

  1. Day 1Arrive and unwindSettle into your resort and spend the afternoon on the beach. Walk the promenade in the evening.
  2. Day 2Timanfaya and El GolfoTake the Ruta de los Volcanes coach tour in Timanfaya National Park. Continue to Los Hervideros, the Salinas de Janubio and the green lagoon at El Golfo.
  3. Day 3Papagayo beachesHave a beach day in the coves of Papagayo. Bring water, food and shade, as facilities are few.
  4. Day 4Manrique’s northVisit the Jardín de Cactus, Jameos del Agua and the Cueva de los Verdes. End the day at the Mirador del Río.
  5. Day 5La GraciosaCatch an early ferry from Órzola to La Graciosa and explore by bike or on foot. Have grilled fish in Caleta de Sebo before the return crossing.
  6. Day 6Teguise, Famara and La GeriaOn a Sunday, browse the Teguise market; otherwise, wander the old town. Continue to Famara beach, then taste Malvasía wine in La Geria.
  7. Day 7Arrecife and a slow last daySee the Fundación César Manrique in the morning and the Charco de San Ginés in Arrecife. Spend the afternoon on the beach.
