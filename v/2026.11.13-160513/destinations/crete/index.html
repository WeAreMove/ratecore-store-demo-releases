About Crete

Crete is the largest Greek island, about 260 km from east to west, with high mountains running down its spine. The White Mountains in the west and Mount Ida, or Psiloritis, at 2,456 m in the centre hold snow into spring. Gorges cut down from them to the Libyan Sea on the south coast, while the north coast has the main towns, airports and resorts.

This is where Europe’s first great civilisation, the Minoans, built palaces such as Knossos and Phaistos about 4,000 years ago. Later came Romans, Byzantines, Venetians and Ottomans, before Crete joined Greece in 1913. The Venetian harbours of Chania and Rethymno and the walls of Heraklion are reminders of that long history.

Crete is big enough for very different holidays. The north coast resorts near Heraklion suit families and nightlife, Chania and Rethymno suit culture and food, and the quieter south coast suits walkers and anyone looking for small villages. Cretan food, built on olive oil, wild greens, cheese and raki, is a reason to go on its own.

At a glance

  • Crete is the largest of the Greek islands, about 260 km long, and the fifth-largest island in the Mediterranean.
  • Crete was the centre of the Bronze Age Minoan civilisation, whose largest palace, Knossos, lies about 5 km south of Heraklion.
  • Direct flights from London to Crete take about 4 hours 5 minutes, landing at Heraklion or Chania.
  • Crete’s highest mountain, Mount Ida (Psiloritis), rises to 2,456 m and is often snow-capped in winter.
  • The Samaria Gorge in western Crete is a national park with a one-way hiking trail of about 16 km, open from around May to October.

When to go to Crete

The best time to visit Crete is May–June or September–October, when it is warm, the sea is swimmable and the gorges are open. July and August are hottest and busiest, with strong meltemi winds on the north coast. Winter is mild on the coast but wet, with snow on the mountains.

Best things to do in Crete

  1. 011. Visit Knossos and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum

    Knossos, about 5 km south of Heraklion, is the largest Minoan palace, with partly reconstructed halls, staircases and frescoes. See it early, before the heat and the tour groups. Then visit the Heraklion Archaeological Museum, which holds the finest Minoan finds, including the Phaistos Disc.

  2. 022. Hike the Samaria Gorge

    The Samaria Gorge, in the White Mountains of western Crete, is a national park with a trail of about 16 km from Xyloskalo down to Agia Roumeli on the south coast. Most people take 5–7 hours. It is usually open from around May to October; you return by boat and bus.

  3. 033. Wander the old town and harbour of Chania

    Chania’s old town wraps around a Venetian harbour with a stone lighthouse, arsenals and the Küçük Hasan Mosque. Narrow lanes behind it are full of houses, workshops and tavernas. The covered market is a good place to buy cheese, honey and herbs.

  4. 044. Swim at Balos Lagoon

    Balos is a shallow lagoon of turquoise water and white sand on the Gramvousa peninsula in north-west Crete. Boats from Kissamos port also stop at Gramvousa island and its Venetian fort; you can also drive a rough track and walk down. It is very busy in summer.

  5. 055. Spend a day at Elafonisi

    Elafonisi, in the far south-west of Crete, is a protected beach and islet joined by shallow, warm water and sand tinged pink by tiny shells. The drive from Chania takes about 1 hour 30 minutes. Do not take sand or shells away.

  6. 066. Take a boat to Spinalonga

    Spinalonga is a small island in the Gulf of Elounda with a Venetian fortress that later became Greece’s leper colony, until 1957. Boats run from Plaka, Elounda and Agios Nikolaos. Its walls and empty streets tell a moving story.

  7. 077. Explore Rethymno’s old town

    Rethymno, halfway between Chania and Heraklion, has a compact Venetian and Ottoman old town with wooden balconies, fountains and minarets. Above it stands the Fortezza, a large 16th-century Venetian fortress with views over the sea.

  8. 088. Visit Arkadi Monastery

    Arkadi Monastery, about 23 km south-east of Rethymno, is a symbol of the Cretan struggle for freedom. In 1866 its defenders blew up the powder store rather than surrender to Ottoman forces. Its Venetian-era church façade is one of the finest on the island.

Hidden gems

  • Imbros GorgeThe Imbros Gorge, near Chora Sfakion on the south coast, is a shorter alternative to Samaria, about 8 km and 2–3 hours downhill. It has narrow passages only a couple of metres wide and is open most of the year.
  • Preveli palm beachPreveli beach, on Crete’s south coast below Preveli Monastery, sits where a river meets the sea, lined with a grove of Cretan date palms. You reach it by a steep path or by boat from Plakias and Agia Galini.
  • Panagia Kera at KritsaPanagia Kera is a small Byzantine church just outside the village of Kritsa, near Agios Nikolaos, covered inside in 13th- and 14th-century frescoes. Kritsa itself is a hill village known for weaving and embroidery.

    Elounda, on the Gulf of Mirabello in eastern Crete, is home to many of the island’s high-end resort hotels, with calm water and views of Spinalonga. It is quiet in the evenings.

  • Plakias

    Good for: WalkersQuiet staysBudget

    Plakias is a small resort on Crete’s south coast, in a bay below steep hills, with a long beach and walking trails to Preveli and nearby villages. It is relaxed and good value.

    Good for: NightlifeFamiliesBudget

    Hersonissos and Malia are busy resorts east of Heraklion, with sandy beaches, water parks and lively nightlife. Malia also has a Minoan palace site. Quieter hotels lie along the coast in between.

Food and drink in Crete

Cretan cooking is the classic Mediterranean diet: olive oil, vegetables, wild greens, pulses, cheese and a little meat. Many tavernas bring a free glass of raki and something sweet at the end of the meal.

Dishes to try

Dakos
A barley rusk topped with grated tomato, crumbled soft cheese, olive oil and oregano. It is Crete’s signature starter.
Kalitsounia
Small pies or pastries filled with soft white cheese such as mizithra, or with wild greens and herbs.
Staka
A rich cream made from sheep’s or goats’ milk, often cooked with eggs or served with fried potatoes.
Gamopilafo
‘Wedding pilaf’: rice cooked slowly in a rich meat broth and finished with staka butter and lemon.
Chochlioi boubouristi
Snails fried face down in olive oil with salt, then finished with vinegar and rosemary.
Graviera
Crete’s hard, nutty sheep’s milk cheese, which has protected status, eaten on its own or fried as saganaki.

What to drink

Tsikoudia (raki)
A clear spirit distilled from grape pomace, drunk in small glasses as a welcome or after a meal.
Cretan wine
Crete has several protected wine areas, including Peza, Archanes and Sitia, with local grapes such as Vidiano, Vilana, Liatiko and Kotsifali.
Malotira
Cretan mountain tea, a herbal infusion made from a wild ironwort that grows at altitude.

Culture and etiquette

Cretans are proud of their island’s history, music and hospitality, and many identify as Cretan first. Village life, family and the Orthodox Church remain central.

  • Accept a glass of raki or a small treat if it is offered; it is a gesture of hospitality.
  • Cover your shoulders and knees when you visit a church or monastery, including Arkadi and Preveli.
  • Dinner is late, often after 9 pm, and meals are long and shared.
  • Greek Easter is the biggest festival of the year and often falls on a different date from Easter in the UK.
  • Do not take sand, shells or stones from protected beaches such as Elafonisi and Balos.

Getting to and around Crete

Airports

  • HERHeraklion International Airport “Nikos Kazantzakis”On the coast about 5 km east of Heraklion. Transfers take about 15 minutes to Heraklion, 30 minutes to Hersonissos, 40 minutes to Malia, 1 hour to Elounda and 1 hour 15 minutes to Rethymno.
  • CHQChania International Airport “Ioannis Daskalogiannis”On the Akrotiri peninsula, about 15 km from Chania. Transfers take about 25 minutes to Chania and 1 hour 15 minutes to Rethymno.

Getting around

Buses
KTEL buses link Chania, Rethymno, Heraklion and Agios Nikolaos along the north coast, with connections to the south coast and to the Samaria Gorge in season. They are reliable and good value.
Car rental
Driving is on the right. A car is the best way to explore Crete’s beaches and villages. The north coast road is busy and some mountain roads are narrow and winding, so allow plenty of time.
Taxis
Taxis are available at both airports and in towns. Agree the fare or check that the meter is running before you set off.
Boats
Ferries link the south-coast villages of Sfakia, Loutro, Agia Roumeli, Sougia and Paleochora, which have no road between them. Day boats run to Balos, Gramvousa and Spinalonga, and overnight ferries to Piraeus near Athens.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can visit Greece for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before you arrive and be valid for at least 3 months after you leave. The EU’s Entry/Exit System records non-EU travellers’ fingerprints and photo at the border, so allow extra time. EU citizens can travel with a valid passport or national identity card. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    Greece uses the euro. Cards are widely accepted in towns and resorts, but carry cash for villages, buses and small tavernas. Hotels also charge a nightly climate resilience fee set by the Greek government, paid locally.

  • Plugs

    Greece uses type C and F two-pin plugs at 230 V, 50 Hz. UK travellers need a plug adaptor.

  • Tipping

    Tipping is not compulsory. Rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service in a restaurant is normal, in cash if possible.

  • Safety

    Crete has low crime. The main risks are road accidents, so drive carefully, and heat on gorge walks: start early and carry plenty of water. Follow local advice on wildfires in summer. The emergency number is 112.

  • Health

    UK travellers should carry a GHIC (or a valid EHIC), which covers state healthcare in Greece, and also take out travel insurance with medical cover, including hiking if you plan to walk the gorges. The summer sun is strong.

  • Water

    Tap water in Crete is treated and generally safe in the main towns, but quality and taste vary between areas and many visitors drink bottled water. Ask at your hotel.

  • Accessibility

    The old towns of Chania and Rethymno have cobbled and uneven lanes, and many archaeological sites have rough paths and steps. Large north-coast resort hotels are usually the most accessible, and some beaches have ramps or sea-access chairs. Ask your hotel about access before booking.

Crete in 7 days

A week from east to west: Minoan sites, Venetian towns, a gorge walk and the best beaches.

  1. Day 1Heraklion and KnossosSee Knossos in the morning and the Heraklion Archaeological Museum in the afternoon.
