About Antalya

Antalya sits on a long bay on Turkey’s south coast, with the Taurus Mountains rising behind it. The walled old town, Kaleiçi, runs down to a small Roman harbour, while two long beaches stretch out on either side of the city: pebbly Konyaaltı to the west and sandy Lara to the east.

The city is the main gateway to the Turkish Riviera, the coast of resorts that runs from Kemer in the west to Belek, Side and Alanya in the east. Many visitors stay in all-inclusive hotels, but Antalya rewards people who go out exploring. Within an hour or so you can reach Aspendos, Perge and Termessos, three of the best-preserved ancient cities in the country.

Summers are long, hot and dry, and the sea stays above 20°C from May to November. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable times for sightseeing, while winter is mild but quite wet. Turkey uses the Turkish lira, and UK visitors can currently stay for up to 90 days without a visa.

When to go to Antalya

The best time to visit Antalya is April to June and September to October, when it is warm, dry and the sea is good for swimming. July and August are the hottest and busiest months, with highs around 35°C. Winter is mild but brings most of the year’s rain.

Best things to do in Antalya

  1. 011. Walk the lanes of Kaleiçi, the old town

    Kaleiçi is Antalya’s walled old town, a maze of lanes with restored Ottoman houses that drops down to the old Roman harbour. Enter through Hadrian’s Gate, built for the emperor’s visit in 130 AD, and look out for the fluted Yivli Minare and the Hıdırlık Tower above the sea.

  2. 022. See the Roman theatre at Aspendos

    The theatre at Aspendos, about 45 km east of Antalya, was built in the 2nd century AD and is one of the best-preserved Roman theatres anywhere. The stage building still stands to its full height, and the site also has the remains of a long Roman aqueduct. Go early in the morning in summer.

  3. 033. Watch the Lower Düden Waterfalls drop into the sea

    At the Lower Düden Waterfalls, the Düden river falls about 40 m off the cliffs straight into the Mediterranean near Lara. A clifftop park gives free views, and boat trips from the Kaleiçi harbour pass underneath the falls. The Upper Düden Waterfalls, in a shady park north of the city, are a good pair.

  4. 044. Explore the Antalya Museum

    The Antalya Museum is one of Turkey’s major archaeological museums, with finds from across the region. Its hall of Roman statues from Perge is the highlight, including the Weary Herakles, whose upper half was returned to Turkey in 2011. Allow two hours, and combine it with Konyaaltı beach next door.

  5. 055. Walk the colonnaded streets of Perge

    Perge, about 18 km east of Antalya, was one of the main cities of ancient Pamphylia. You can walk its long colonnaded street, still lined with columns, and see the Hellenistic towers of the city gate, the baths and the large stadium. The site is flat but open, so bring water and a hat.

  6. 066. Climb to Termessos in the mountains

    Termessos is a ruined city at about 1,000 m in the mountains about 35 km north-west of Antalya, inside a national park. Alexander the Great failed to take it in 333 BC. Its small theatre looks over a deep valley, and the walk up from the car park takes about 20–30 minutes on rough paths.

  7. 077. See the flames of the Chimaera at Olympos

    At Yanartaş, above the village of Çıralı about 80 km south-west of Antalya, small flames burn from gas vents in the rocks and have done so since ancient times. The walk up takes about 20–30 minutes and is best at dusk. Combine it with the overgrown ruins and long beach of ancient Olympos nearby.

  8. 088. Raft through Köprülü Canyon

    Köprülü Canyon National Park, about 90 km north-east of Antalya, is the area’s best-known place for gentle white-water rafting on the Köprüçay river. Most trips suit beginners, and minimum ages vary by operator. A Roman bridge still crosses the gorge, and the ruins of Selge sit higher up.

Hidden gems

  • Phaselis, the city of three harboursPhaselis is an ancient Lycian port between Kemer and Tekirova, set among pine trees with three small harbours where you can swim. A paved Roman street links them, and it is far quieter than the ruins closer to Antalya.
  • Karain CaveKarain Cave, about 27 km north-west of Antalya, is one of the oldest known inhabited caves in Turkey, used since the Palaeolithic. A short, steep path leads to the chambers, and a small museum at the site shows tools found inside.
  • Tünektepe cable carThe Tünektepe cable car climbs from the western end of Konyaaltı to a hilltop at about 600 m. The view takes in the whole bay of Antalya and the Beydağları mountains, and it is at its best at sunset.

Where to stay in Antalya

  • Kaleiçi

    Good for: CultureCouplesFood lovers

    Kaleiçi, the walled old town, is the place for character: small boutique hotels in old Ottoman houses, restaurants in courtyards and the harbour on the doorstep. Lanes are steep and cobbled, and there is no long beach, only small bathing spots below the walls.

  • Konyaaltı

    Good for: BeachesFamiliesBudget

    Konyaaltı is a 7 km pebble beach west of the centre, backed by a landscaped promenade with cafés, play areas and the Antalya Museum. It has city hotels of every level and views of the mountains across the bay.

  • Kemer

    Good for: WalkersNightlifeFamilies

    Kemer, about 45 km south-west of Antalya, is a resort town between the mountains and a pebbly coast, with a marina and lively bars. It is the best base for Phaselis, Olympos and walks in the Beydağları Coastal National Park.

  • Lara

    Good for: FamiliesBeachesLuxury

    Lara, east of the city and about 15 minutes from the airport, is the area for large all-inclusive resorts on a long sandy beach. The Lower Düden Waterfalls are at its western end.

  • Belek

    Good for: LuxuryFamiliesQuiet stays

    Belek, about 35 km east of Antalya, is a strip of big resort hotels along a sandy beach, known for its golf courses. It is quiet, self-contained and close to Aspendos and Perge.

Food and drink in Antalya

Antalya’s food is Turkish Mediterranean: grilled meat, vegetables and olive oil, with a few local dishes built on tahini. Breakfast is a meal in itself, and meze come before most dinners.

Dishes to try

Antalya piyazı
The city’s own white bean salad, dressed with tahini, lemon, garlic and vinegar. It is almost always served with şiş köfte.
Şiş köfte
Spiced minced meat grilled on flat skewers over charcoal, served with flatbread, grilled peppers and tomatoes.
Hibeş
A sharp tahini dip with garlic, lemon, cumin and chilli flakes, a speciality of the Antalya area eaten as part of a meze.
Tandır kebabı
Lamb slow-cooked for hours in a pit oven until it falls apart, a traditional dish of the Antalya region.
Gözleme
Thin hand-rolled dough folded around spinach, cheese or potato and cooked on a griddle, often made in front of you at village stops and markets.
Kahvaltı
A Turkish breakfast spread of white cheese, olives, tomatoes, cucumbers, eggs, honey, jams and fresh bread, with endless tea.

What to drink

Çay
Black tea served in small tulip-shaped glasses, drunk all day and offered as a welcome in shops.
Ayran
A cold, salty yoghurt drink that goes with grilled meat and kebabs.
Rakı
An aniseed spirit that turns milky white with water, drunk slowly with meze and fish.

Culture and etiquette

Antalya is relaxed and used to visitors, but Turkey is a mainly Muslim country with a strong sense of hospitality and national pride. A little care goes a long way.

  • In mosques, cover your shoulders and knees, take off your shoes, and women should cover their hair with a scarf.
  • Bargaining is normal in bazaars and markets, but not in restaurants, supermarkets or shops with marked prices.
  • Insulting Atatürk, the Turkish flag or the Turkish nation is a criminal offence, so avoid jokes about them.
  • If a shopkeeper offers you tea, it is a friendly gesture and fine to accept without any obligation to buy.
  • Ask before you photograph people, and do not photograph military or police sites.

Getting to and around Antalya

Airports

  • AYTAntalya AirportAntalya Airport is about 13 km east of the city centre. By road it takes about 15–20 minutes to Lara, 20–30 minutes to Kaleiçi and Konyaaltı, about 30–40 minutes to Belek, about 1 hour to Kemer and about 1 hour 15 minutes to Side.
  • GZPGazipaşa–Alanya AirportGazipaşa–Alanya Airport is a smaller airport about 40 km south-east of Alanya, with fewer international flights. It is only worth using if you are staying in or near Alanya.

Getting around

Tram and buses
The AntRay tram links the airport area, the city centre and the bus station, and city buses serve Konyaaltı and Lara. Pay with an Antalyakart travel card, sold at kiosks and machines.
Dolmuş minibuses
Shared dolmuş minibuses run between Antalya and towns such as Kemer, Belek and Side from the main bus station. They are cheap and frequent, and you can flag them down along the route.
Car rental
Hiring a car makes day trips to Termessos, Olympos and Köprülü Canyon easy. Turkey drives on the right, main roads are good, and mountain roads are winding, so allow extra time.
Taxis
Yellow taxis use meters within the city. For long trips, such as to Kemer or Side, agree the fare before you set off.

Practical information

  • Entry requirements

    UK passport holders can currently visit Turkey for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa, and your passport should be valid for at least 150 days from the day you arrive. Many EU citizens can also enter visa-free, some with a national ID card, but the rules depend on nationality. Check GOV.UK foreign travel advice before you travel.

  • Money

    The currency is the Turkish lira (TRY), and cards are widely accepted in Antalya and the resorts. Prices can change quickly because of high inflation, and some places quote in euros. When a card machine offers to charge you in pounds, choose lira for a better rate.

  • Plugs and power

    Turkey uses type C and F plugs with two round pins, at 230 V and 50 Hz. UK visitors need a travel adaptor.

  • Tipping

    Leaving around 10% in restaurants is usual if service is not already included. Rounding up taxi fares and giving hotel staff a small tip is appreciated.

  • Safety

    Antalya is generally safe for visitors, and serious crime against tourists is rare. Watch your belongings in busy places, take care in the summer heat, and note that wildfires can affect the region in hot, dry months.

  • Health

    The UK GHIC is not valid in Turkey, so take out travel insurance with good medical cover before you go. Visitors usually use private hospitals and clinics. In summer, drink plenty of water and avoid the midday sun.

  • Drinking water

    Tap water in Antalya is treated but very hard, and most locals and visitors drink bottled water. Hotels and restaurants serve bottled water as standard.

  • Accessibility

    Kaleiçi has steep, cobbled lanes and steps down to the harbour, which are hard going with a wheelchair or pushchair. The Konyaaltı promenade is flat and step-free, and large resort hotels are usually accessible. Ancient sites have uneven ground with few ramps.

Antalya itineraries

3 days

Antalya in 3 days

A short break that covers the old town, two great Roman sites and a beach afternoon.

  1. Day 1Kaleiçi and the old harbourEnter Kaleiçi through Hadrian’s Gate and wander down past the Yivli Minare to the old harbour. Take a short boat trip to see the Lower Düden Waterfalls from the sea, then watch the sunset from the Hıdırlık Tower.
  2. Day 2Perge and AspendosLeave early for Perge, then continue to the theatre at Aspendos before the midday heat. Stop at the Lower Düden Waterfalls park on the way back.
  3. Day 3Antalya Museum and KonyaaltıSpend the morning in the Antalya Museum, then cross to Konyaaltı beach for a swim. End the day on the Tünektepe cable car for views over the whole bay.
7 days

Antalya in 7 days

A week that mixes beaches with ancient cities, mountains and the coast towards Kemer.

  1. Day 1KaleiçiSettle in with a slow walk round the old town, its walls and the harbour. Have dinner in one of the courtyard restaurants.
  2. Day 2Antalya Museum and KonyaaltıSee the statues from Perge at the Antalya Museum, then spend the afternoon on Konyaaltı beach. Take the Tünektepe cable car at sunset.
  3. Day 3Perge and AspendosVisit the colonnaded streets of Perge and the Roman theatre at Aspendos. Return via the Lower Düden Waterfalls.
  4. Day 4Termessos and Karain CaveDrive into the mountains to Termessos and climb to its theatre in the cool of the morning. Stop at Karain Cave on the way back.
  5. Day 5Köprülü CanyonSpend the day rafting in Köprülü Canyon National Park, or walk across the Roman bridge and up towards Selge. Expect a long day out.
  6. Day 6Phaselis and the ChimaeraHead south-west to swim in the harbours of Phaselis, then continue to Çıralı. Walk up to the flames of the Chimaera at dusk.
  7. Day 7Lara beachFinish with a lazy day on the sand at Lara. Have a last dinner back in Kaleiçi.
